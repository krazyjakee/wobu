Making images

The Influence Stack

The panel on the right is the whole product in one column: which layers are contributing to this image, how much each one weighs, and exactly which words each one put in the prompt.

How the stack resolves

Given the node you have selected, Wobu walks outward and collects sources in a fixed order — outermost first, subject last:

# Layer Source Contributes
1 Style Style Guide Medium, rendering, line quality, lighting model, global negatives
2 World World Canon Era, tone, tech and magic level, material vocabulary
3 Ancestry species_of chain Body plan, anatomy, skin and hide, scale
4 Culture member_of chain Costume, ornament, iconography, weapon language
5 Place located_in chain Climate, architecture, ambient light, wear
6 Subject the node itself Everything specific
7 Shot the controls below Framing, pose, output preset, aspect

Chains resolve outermost-first, so a district inherits its city which inherits its region. Cycles are broken by first-visit-wins. Each source collected becomes one card in the Inspector.

Fragments, not paragraphs

A layer doesn't contribute a block of prose — it contributes fragments. Each fragment knows its source node, which description section it came from, its weight, and where it should be routed: 

prompt · negative · style_ref · structure_ref · palette · moodboard_only

A fragment's weight is the product of three things:

link weight  ×  section priority (per preset)  ×  your slider

Section priority is why presets matter. A material study boosts materials and drops silhouette; a turnaround does the reverse. The same description produces a different prompt depending on what kind of sheet you asked for.

Compiling

  1. Resolve and collect. Walk the stack, gather every fragment.
  2. Filter. Drop muted layers and anything marked mood-board-only.
  3. Score and sort by weight, stable within layer order.
  4. Budget. Trim to the model's usable prompt length, dropping the lowest-weight fragments first — and report what was dropped rather than truncating in silence. Images are budgeted separately and more tightly, per role bucket, against the backend's declared limits.
  5. Emit. Positive prompt joined by layer with the subject last; negative prompt built from every layer's Never section; style references to style transfer, structure references to ControlNet, palettes to colour conditioning; then model, aspect, steps, seed and any LoRAs the Style Guide pinned.
  6. Snapshot. The entire resolved stack — every weight, every mute, every dropped fragment — is saved with the generation.

Reading the compiled prompt

Every fragment keeps its layer identity all the way to the screen, which is what lets the compiled-prompt box tint each span by origin:

painterly gouache, heavy brush texture, strong single key light, deep shadow · late iron age, forty years post-eruption, scavenged and repaired materials · tall narrow-shouldered digitigrade humanoid, four-jointed legs, ash-grey hide · ember guild kiln-glaze plate over oiled leather, rope fastenings, collarbone signet · cinder bay harbour light, salt-bleached timber, ash haze · Kael Vantris — armour cut for a broader frame, cinched with rope; burn scar left jaw to temple; signet ground flat; unlit ashglass lantern at the belt

Attribution is not a debug feature. It is the main feedback loop for learning to write good upstream notes. When an image comes out wrong, the tint tells you which node to go and fix — and over a few weeks it teaches you what belongs at which altitude far more effectively than any documentation could.

Layer cards

Each card in the stack gives you:

Colour dot & layer name Which of the seven layers this is.
Source node Clickable — jumps to that node so you can edit it.
Fragment & reference count How much this layer is actually contributing, including anything dropped by budget.
Weight slider Scales every fragment from this layer.
Mute toggle Removes the layer from this generation entirely.
Expander The exact text and images it contributes, verbatim.
Inspector edits are per-generation

Muting and reweighting here affect this generation only — they never edit the world. That separation is deliberate and absolute. If you find yourself muting the same layer on every render, that's not a workflow, that's a signal: go change the node, or change the link.

Link weights, by contrast, are part of the world. Set them from the node's Relations tab when a character should genuinely be only loosely of its culture.

Practical steering

Style is overpowering the subject Drop the Style layer to ~0.6 for this shot. If it happens constantly, your Style Guide notes are too specific — move detail down to World or Culture.
Character keeps looking generic Check the Subject layer's fragment count. A thin subject layer usually means the notes are backstory rather than description — Enhance drops anything not visible on the body.
Two characters look identical Their subject layers aren't saying enough that differs. The species and culture layers are doing all the work, which is exactly what they're supposed to do.
Something anachronistic keeps appearing Add it to Never on the highest layer where it's always wrong — usually World Canon. It compiles into the negative prompt for every descendant.
Fragments are being dropped You're over prompt budget. Mute a layer you don't need for this shot, or find the duplication — usually a child restating what its parent already said.
A reference isn't being used Check its role. mood is never sent anywhere, and structure roles are inert on backends without ControlNet.

The snapshot

Every generation stores its influence_snapshot — the exact resolved stack, weights and all, at the moment you pressed Generate. That is what makes a result reproducible six months later, after the world has moved on and three of those layers have been rewritten. It is also what makes generation history genuinely replayable rather than just a log of images.
