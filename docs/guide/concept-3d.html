Making images

Concept 3D

Eight views of one subject, reviewed, then reconstructed into a mesh you can turn around in the app and hand to a modeller.

The pipeline

  1. Generate a turnaround. Pick the Turnaround preset on a character, creature or prop. It emits eight views on a locked seed. The 3D tab (⌘4) will also start one for you if the subject has never had one.
  2. Review the sheet. The 3D tab shows the turnaround as a set of eight slots. Views that came out inconsistent can be re-rolled individually — one image, on a seed of its own — before you spend money on reconstruction. Re-roll as often as you like; every take is kept, and each slot lets you go back to an earlier one.
  3. Reconstruct. Choose the face count and reconstruction mode, confirm the cost, and press Reconstruct. Wobu submits the views to the mesh backend and polls the job; Stop cancels it like any other. A .glb lands in the project folder when it's done.
  4. Inspect and export. The finished mesh appears in the viewer beside the sheet — an inline three.js turntable with a wireframe toggle and a one-metre marker — and exports for a modeller from the same toolbar.
The sheet is the last cheap place to fix a mesh

Reconstruction is one paid job and takes minutes. A bad back view is seconds to re-roll and cannot be repaired afterwards, so Wobu will not let you start until every view the backend needs is on screen and chosen.

Why the Turnaround preset looks the way it does

Turnaround is not a generic "spin the character" preset. It exists to feed a specific 3D backend whose headline capability is eight-view reconstruction, so it emits exactly the view types that backend names: 

front · left · right · back · top · bottom · left_front · right_front

Each view is one generation with a locked seed and a framing fragment appended, tagged with its view type — so the mesh stage can hand them straight over with no intermediate mapping. That is a much stronger pipeline than single-image-to-3D, and it falls out of the influence engine for free.

Constraints are baked into the preset

The backend accepts PNG and JPEG only for multi-view, needs a minimum side of 128px, and caps the whole payload at 6 MB before encoding. It also wants a plain background, a single object, and the subject filling more than half the frame.

All of that lives in the preset's framing fragments and output settings, so a turnaround can never be generated in a form the 3D stage would reject. You find out at generation time, not after paying for eight images.

Text does not reach this stage

The 3.1 mesh model has no text-and-image conditioning path — image-to-3D and text-to-3D are mutually exclusive. So the compiled prompt does not ride along to the mesh stage.

In practice this costs nothing, because by the time you reach 3D all of your influence has already been baked into the turnaround images themselves. But it does mean the mesh is only ever as good as the views: fix the turnaround before you reconstruct. An inconsistent back view produces a bad mesh, and no amount of prompt work at this stage will help.

Jobs and waiting

The backend protocol is asynchronous — submit, then poll — and a reconstruction is a job in the same queue as images, with the same Stop button and the same progress line. The queue holds the backend to three concurrent jobs because that is the account limit.

A mesh job is minutes long, which means most of the ways it can fail happen after the money was spent. Wobu writes a receipt for those too, marked as failed and carrying what it cost, so a job that produced nothing still leaves a record you can find.

Provider results expire within 24 hours, so Wobu downloads the finished mesh into the project folder rather than keeping a URL that would rot. It appears in the viewer as soon as it lands.

What a reconstruction costs

The hosted backend charges per submitted job — including one you cancel while it is running — and its international API does not report the amount back. There is therefore no honest figure Wobu can put in front of you and nothing for the image spend ceiling to reserve, so paid reconstruction is gated on a confirmation instead: you tick the box that says so, and the receipt records how many jobs the provider admitted to billing.

Storage

assets/meshes/7b/7b21…04.glb

Content-addressed like every other asset, so meshes dedupe and never conflict on a shared folder. They are also lazy: a .glb is only pulled off the share when you actually open the 3D tab, because a mesh is orders of magnitude larger than a thumbnail and most sessions never need one.

Local versus hosted

You pick the 3D backend in Settings, per project. Local ComfyUI mesh generation is an explicit alternative to the hosted service, attractive for cost and privacy, and it carries a quality trade:

Local is a different tier, not a fallback

Open weights stop at an earlier version than the hosted model, and that version reconstructs from a single image rather than eight. Running locally gets you a materially older and lower-quality result, not a drop-in substitute for the cloud one. Wobu presents it as a separate quality tier and won't silently fall back to it when a key is missing.

When the selected backend takes fewer views than a turnaround has, the 3D tab says which ones it will actually send — the front view first, because a single-image reconstruction is the front view — and dims the rest rather than hiding them.

What you get out

A blockout mesh with optional PBR materials, at a face count you choose. These are concept geometry: enough to judge proportion, silhouette and scale in three dimensions, enough to hand to a modeller as a reference, and not enough to ship.
